# Stitch prompts — site header, in three small batches

The single 8-design prompt returned 2. Same content, split into three runs of
3. Run them **separately** — one paste each, in any order.

Everything above the line is for you, not for Stitch.

---

## BATCH A — designs 1 to 3

Design **only the header** of a local garage-door repair company's website —
the bar at the very top of every page. **Nothing else: no hero, no page
sections, no footer.**

Give me **3 designs**, numbered 1 to 3. Show each one **three times**:
desktop at 1280px with one dropdown open, mobile at 390px closed, and mobile
at 390px with the menu drawer open.

Make these three differ by **where the navigation sits**: one with nav in the
middle, one with nav pushed hard to the right, one with nav on its own second
row below the company name.

### Every design must contain all of this

- **A thin announcement strip across the very top** with two short lines:
  *"Serving Dallas & the surrounding metro"* and *"Same-day service
  available"*. Required in all three. It may be styled or aligned differently
  but not removed.
- **The company lockup**: small square logo mark, the name *"Dallas Garage
  Door Co"*, and a tagline beneath, *"Garage Door Repair & Installation"*.
- **Three dropdown menus** — Services, Service Areas, Guides — plus plain
  links *About* and *Contact*.
- **One button**: *"Free Quote"*.

### The dropdowns are the hard part

Services holds about 6 links. **Service Areas holds up to 18**, and some are
long, like *"Buckner Terrace / Everglade Park"*. Show the Service Areas
dropdown open in at least one design. Long names must **wrap onto two lines —
never be cut off with "…"**. Multiple columns inside the dropdown are welcome.

### Rules

- The header **sticks to the top** while the page scrolls. Keep the sticky
  part under **90px on desktop, 60px on mobile**. The announcement strip may
  scroll away.
- The company name can be long — *"Punta Gorda Garage Door Pros"* — and must
  never be truncated or overlap the navigation.
- Tap targets at least 44px on mobile. No horizontal scrolling at 390px.
- Light page background. Say if a design uses a dark bar.

### Do not include — we have none of this data

Search box, cart, login, language switcher, star ratings, review counts,
"trusted by" logos, certification badges, social icons, live chat, booking
calendar, opening hours, email address, street address, and **any number or
statistic** ("24/7", "500+ jobs", "15 years").

**No phone number in any of these three.** Fewer than 1% of the sites have
one.

Real placeholder text, not "Lorem ipsum". One line per design saying what
makes it structurally different.

## BATCH B — designs 4 to 6

*Same brief as Batch A above — repeat the content list, the dropdown rules,
the rules, and the do-not-include list exactly.* Change only this:

Give me **3 designs, numbered 4 to 6**, different from a centred nav, a
right-aligned nav, and a two-row nav.

Make these three differ by **what the bar is made of**: one where the
announcement text moves up into the header bar itself instead of sitting in
its own strip, one built as a full-width dark bar, and one where the logo sits
inside a coloured block that breaks out of the bar's height.

**One of these three may show a phone number**, and it must still look correct
with that element deleted.

---

## BATCH C — designs 7 and 8

*Same brief as Batch A above.* Change only this:

Give me **2 designs, numbered 7 and 8**, different from everything described
in Batches A and B.

Make these two the **most unusual** of the set — but still a working header
for a trade business, not a fashion site. For example: navigation that reads
as a single line of text rather than buttons, or a header split into two
distinct zones with a visible divide.

Both must still hold the announcement strip, the full lockup, the three
dropdowns, About, Contact and the Free Quote button.
